Deployments

A deployment is one trip into the arena: a sector (difficulty 1–5), a goal (survive, extract, escort, capture a node, make the highlight reel), and usually two or three encounters with movement, exploration and checks in between.

The Spectacle Score

Every encounter has a Spectacle Score from 1 to 6: how much the broadcast wants this fight. Roll a d6, or pick one to suit the story. It sets the preset force, any modifiers and part of the payout.

Building an Encounter PLAYTEST

  1. Budget: 4 threat per operator for a solid fight. Use 3 per operator for a warm-up and 6 for a brutal one. Prime-Time doubles damage, so treat it as one step harder.
  2. Spend it on mobs at their threat cost: Fodder 1, Support 2, Standard 2, Tank 4, Elite 4, Boss 8.
  3. Mix roles. One anchor (Tank or Elite), one problem (a Support, Sapper or status effect), then bodies.
  4. Add terrain: cover, a hazard, a camera drone worth playing to.
  5. Set the Spectacle Score for the payout and any modifier.
Example

Four operators: a budget of 16. Rent-Enforcer (Collector) #20 (Standard, 2) + Mainframe-Guardian (Monolith) #28 (Tank, 4) + System-Janitor (Support, 2) + 4 Pixel-Hounds (Fodder, 4) + 2 Ad-Drones (Standard, 4) = 16. The Janitor keeps the Guardian alive while the hounds hunt whoever is lowest. Spectacle 4.

Promoting & demoting

Promote a mob one tier for a named rival or a finale: +8 Integrity, +1 AP, +1 attack die, +2 Defense, +2 threat. The Code-Executioner and Mainframe-Guardian make excellent promoted bosses. Demote for swarms: half Integrity, threat 1.

Running Mobs

  • Mobs act on their Init; run identical mobs as one group.
  • Each turn a mob refills its AP and spends it like an operator: 1 to attack, ability costs as listed, 6 m Move free.
  • Mobs never get Neural Shock. At 0 Integrity they're defeated.
  • Machines and network entities target Glitchers first when the choice is otherwise even.
  • Play to the role: Swarmers flank, Harassers kite, Supports hide behind Tanks, Low-HP Hunters chase the bleeding.
