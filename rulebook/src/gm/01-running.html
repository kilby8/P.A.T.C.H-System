Running the Show

You wear three hats: the arena that tries to break them, the corporation that owns them, and the broadcast that makes them famous. Switch hats often.

In this chapter

Your Three Roles

The arena. Sectors, hazards and the fifty mobs of the Bestiary. Be fair and be relentless; the operators chose to be here, or at least signed something.

The corporation. The terminal, the market, the condo tower, the induction tone. It is never cruel on purpose and never kind by accident. It speaks in ALL CAPS memos.

The broadcast. Producers, sponsors, the crowd and the viewer count. The broadcast is how the operators get paid, so they'll court it, and that is your strongest lever. Reward spectacle. Notice cowardice out loud.

When to Call for a Roll

Call for a check only when failure is possible and interesting. Climbing a ladder isn't a check. Climbing a ladder while a Pixel-Hound chews the bottom rung is. If the operator can simply keep trying with no pressure, let them succeed and spend the time instead.

Name three things every time: attribute + skill, the target, and, if it isn't obvious, what failure costs.

Setting Targets

Pick a rung from the ladder. Don't invent in-between numbers unless you have a reason; fixed rungs are what make bigger pools feel like progress.

A starting operator rolls about 5–6 dice in their specialty (attribute 3–4 plus rank 2) and 3 dice untrained. A veteran rolls 8 to 10. The full odds table is in the Handbook, Skills & Checks.

Situational modifiers

Prefer advantage/disadvantage for circumstances (good tools, bad footing) and +1 die for real help. Moving the target up a rung is for when the task itself is harder, not the conditions.

Pacing a Session

A good session is one deployment plus one Condo Phase, around three to four hours:

  1. Cold open (10 min): a terminal memo, a sponsor ping, a rival's highlight reel.
  2. Deployment (90–150 min): 2–3 encounters in one sector, with a breather between them.
  3. Table break = Condo Hours (15 min): start the app's Condo timer and take a real break.
  4. Condo Phase (30–45 min): terminal, advancement, one scene per player.
  5. Stinger (5 min): something the cameras caught that the players didn't.
