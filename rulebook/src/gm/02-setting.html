The Setting & Its Secrets

The Council is what people think governs them. The Quorum is what the Council answers to. Your players may not learn the second name for a long time.

In this chapter

Three Tiers of Power

TierWhat it isWho knows
The FederationThe member planets themselves: the body of worldsEveryone
The Council of the FederationThe governing representatives: the public, day-to-day face of powerEveryone
The QuorumThe elite few above the Council who actually decide, including greenlighting the showAlmost no one

The Council: pay-to-play representation

Council seats are weighted by a planet's worth: population, exported goods and economic output. Rich, populous core worlds hold many seats. Poor, sparse worlds hold a few, maybe one. It isn't one world, one voice. The core worlds dominate, and small or poor planets are nearly voiceless.

That is why the show matters to the people in it. For a Slum Distrikt volunteer or an Off-Worlder, a season in the arena is one of the few ladders up. Use the Council as the visible villain: hearings about "arena safety", seat counts traded like stock, representatives who are also sponsors.

The Quorum: the inherited inner circle

Quorum membership is inherited by the oldest founding planets, the original worlds that created the Federation. It isn't earned and it isn't bought; wealth won't get you in. It's a hereditary elite of founder worlds sitting above the Council, and the Council answers to it.

Running the conspiracy

Players may not know the Quorum exists. Reveal it in layers:

  1. Council decisions that make no sense on the seat math.
  2. A sponsor rep who defers to "the founders' office".
  3. An operator's Memory Rip or Intel Leak turning up a document with a founder-world seal.
  4. A sector that exists in no Council record, greenlit anyway.
  5. The name itself, said once on a hot mic and never again.

The Program & the Arena

The arena is virtual. Operators jack into a simulated world, like a full-dive game. CPU attributes are their specs inside the system. Gear, damage and blueprints are system-real, and the broadcast is real.

Dying in the arena doesn't kill the person, but the build is erased: character, nodes, installs and stockpile. Use that. Fear of permadeath here is fear of losing months of investment, and that's exactly the corporation's leverage: the operator's labor is the asset at risk.

Sectors are arena zones rated 1 to 5. Difficulty sets the loot table (Chapter 4) and the flavor: sector 1 is scrapyards and abandoned malls rendered in cheap textures; sector 5 is where the simulation forgets what it was rendering.

The corporation that runs the program isn't named in canon yet. It speaks through the terminal, the induction module and the producers. Whether it's one megacorp or a cartel of rivals is your call until the design settles it.

Origins as Story Engines

Each origin has its own reason for being in the arena. Lean on it.

  • Inmate Block: the sentence. Who controls the parole math? What happens when an inmate gets popular?
  • Slum Distrikt Volunteer: the debt and the district. Family watching the feed. A district councilor who wants a photo op.
  • Off-Grid Glitcher: the anomaly. Hostile AI targets them first. Something in the system remembers where they came from.
  • Off-Worlder PLAYTEST: conscripted, or took a deal they didn't understand. They want to go home or earn citizenship, and both goals run straight through the Council, and above it.

The Condo Tower

Every operator of the same megacorp lives in one tower: private units plus shared common areas. Operators opt in to broadcast scene by scene. Teams may live together on air; solo players can stay entirely private. Going dark always costs something you choose on the spot. See Chapter 5.

Open Canvas

These are deliberately undecided. Make them yours, and tell the designer what worked:

  • The corporation's name and whether it has rivals.
  • Where and when the tower and the Slum Distrikts are: Earth, orbit or a colony world.
  • Who outside the program resists it, and how glitchers connect to them.
