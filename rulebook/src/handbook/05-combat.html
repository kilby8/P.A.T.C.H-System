Encounter Flow

  1. Surprise. If one side is sneaking up on the other, the ambushers make a group PING + Stealth check; compare each ambusher's total to the defenders' best SYSTEM + Perception. If at least half the ambushers beat it, every defender who didn't notice them is Surprised: no AP on their first turn.PLAYTEST
  2. Initiative. Everyone's place in the turn order.
  3. Rounds. Each round, everyone takes a turn in initiative order. A round is about six seconds of arena time.
  4. End. When one side is defeated, flees or achieves the scenario goal.
  5. Payout. Overshields reset to full, the GM resolves loot and fragments, and the feed reacts (Ch. 7).

Initiative PLAYTEST

Each operator rolls their PING dice (no skill) and records the total. Each enemy group acts on the Init value in its stat block. Highest goes first; operators win ties. The order holds for the whole encounter.

Your Turn & AP

At the start of your turn you gain AP equal to your maximum (1 + SYSTEM ÷ 2, rounded down), minus 1 if you're in Neural Shock. You also get one free Move of up to 6 meters, which you can split around your actions.

Spend AP on actions in any order. Unspent AP carries over: it pays for reactions on other people's turns, and whatever is left when your next turn starts adds to that turn's AP. You can never hold more than your maximum + 1.PLAYTEST That's how an operator with 1 AP saves up for a 2-AP action, and why holding back is a real choice: a dodge in reserve, or a bigger turn next round.

  • No AP turns. If you're Stunned or Surprised, you gain no AP that turn, but you keep any AP you banked.
  • Bonus AP from abilities (Burst Mode, Kinetic Absorb, Execution, Fan Boost) also can't take you past max + 1.
  • Staggered: gain 1 less AP that turn.
Example

Rook has 3 AP. She attacks once (1 AP) and keeps 2 for Reflex Dodge. Nobody shoots her, so at the start of her next turn she gains 3 more, capped at 4 (her max + 1).

Reactions

A reaction is an action you take outside your turn in response to a trigger: a dodge when you're shot, a counter when a blow misses. Pay for it with saved AP. Unless an ability says otherwise, you can take one reaction per round.PLAYTEST

Every operator has one reaction built into their armor: Counter (1 AP). When a melee attack against you misses, deal your armor's counter damage to the attacker without a roll: Thick Hide Tunic 1d4, Kevlar Vest 1d6, Kinetic Shielding 1d8, each + HARDWARE bonus.

Making an Attack

  1. Choose a weapon and a target in range (weapon tables, p. {{pg:gear-items}}).
  2. Roll the weapon's attribute + skill (Club: POWER + Melee; Bow: PING + Marksmanship) against the target's Defense.
  3. On a hit, roll damage: weapon dice + the attribute's bonus, +1 per full 5 flair. A hit always deals at least 1.
Example

Rook fires the Recurve Bow at a Net-Junkie (Defense 15): PING 4 + Marksmanship 2 = 6d6. She rolls 22, a hit with 7 flair. Damage is 1d8 + 1 (PING bonus) + 1 (one full 5 of flair). She rolls a 5 on the d8 for 7 damage, and the Net-Junkie's 10 Integrity drops to 3.

Range

Melee reaches 1 meter (the Iron Spear 2). A ranged weapon hits normally out to its range, and out to double range with disadvantage. A ranged attack made while an enemy is in melee with you also has disadvantage.

Cover

Half cover (a crate, a railing): +2 Defense. Heavy cover (an arrow slit, a wall corner): +5 Defense. Full cover: you can't be targeted directly. The Hard-Light Lance ignores non-energy cover.PLAYTEST

Damage & Vitals

You have two layers of health.

Overshield takes damage first. When it hits 0, the rest of that damage, and all further damage, goes to Hardware Integrity. Some attacks bypass Overshield and hit Hardware directly; they say so.

Damage types. Physical damage is blunt, piercing and kinetic. Non-physical damage is energy, heat, signal, disruption and utility. Effects that name a type affect only that type.

Damage reduction from blueprints and sub-skills ("reduce physical damage by 1") applies to each hit before the split, and can lower a hit to 0.

Recovering

Overshield regenerates freely: use the Reboot Shield action in a fight, and it resets to full at the end of every encounter. Hardware Integrity is the hard part. It comes back only through Condo Phase recovery (25% of max per condo hour, Ch. 8), the Patch Up action, and abilities such as Field Weld.

The feed loves pain

Every point of Overshield damage you take is +1 Engagement, and every point of Hardware damage is +2. Taking hits on camera is how a lot of operators get famous. See Chapter 7.

Neural Shock

When your Hardware Integrity drops below 50% of its maximum, you enter Neural Shock: you start each turn with 1 less AP (minimum 0). It ends as soon as Hardware climbs back to 50% or more. The Titan Chassis sub-skill Redundant Systems lowers your threshold to 35%.

Flatlined & Permadeath

At 0 Hardware Integrity you are Flatlined: you drop, you have no AP and you can't act. Damage beyond 0 is ignored.

Crash saves.PLAYTEST At the start of each of your turns while Flatlined, roll 1d6: 4–6 is a success, 1–3 a failure. A 6 counts as two successes; a 1 counts as two failures.

  • Three successes: you're stable. You stay down until the encounter ends, then get up with 1 Hardware Integrity.
  • Three failures: the operator is destroyed.
  • Taking damage while Flatlined counts as one failure.
  • Any healing brings you back up with that much Hardware Integrity, and you act normally on your next turn.
  • An ally can Stabilize you (1 AP, SYSTEM + Medicine against 15).
What destroyed means

The person who jacked in wakes up. The build does not. The operator, unlocked nodes and sub-skills, installed blueprints and the blueprint stockpile are erased. Make a new operator; the program is always recruiting.

Replacement operators PLAYTEST

A new operator joining a running season uses normal creation, plus:

  • Advancement Points equal to the lowest total any surviving squadmate has earned (minimum 2).
  • +20 Data Fragments per deployment the squad has completed (maximum +200).
  • One Foundation blueprint of your choice in the stockpile.

The arena keeps your old operator's highlight reel. The new one starts at their origin's normal Engagement.

Stealth

While Stealthed, enemies can't target you with attacks or abilities. Your first attack from Stealth has advantage, and then you're revealed (some sub-skills, like Persistent Ghost, change that). Enemies can search: their Perception against your Hide total.

Spectacle & Prime Time

Every encounter has a Spectacle Score from 1 to 6 that tells the GM how big the broadcast wants it. At the top of the scale, a Prime-Time Surge, enemy damage is doubled, and the fragment payout is the biggest a fight can pay. You'll usually hear the crowd before you see it.
