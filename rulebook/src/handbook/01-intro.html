Welcome to the Program

You are not merely a soldier. You are a mobile testing platform, a reality-TV contestant, and a line item on a megacorp's R&D budget. The cameras are always on.

The Show

P.A.T.C.H. is a cyber-gladiatorial tabletop roleplaying game about corporate dystopia, live broadcast and permanent choices.

The Program for Accelerated Tactical Combat Hazards sends private military operators into hostile arena sectors to field-test experimental gear. Every fight generates telemetry. Every death generates telemetry. The corporation that runs the program turns that data into next season's product line, and it broadcasts the whole thing as a live combat sport with an audience, sponsors and a ratings meter.

You play one of those operators. You fight, you salvage blueprints from the wreckage, you bolt them permanently onto your gear, and you deploy again, stronger and stranger. Or you don't come back.

Three ideas hold the game together

  • Genre and tone: cyberpunk crossed with gladiatorial reality TV. Media-saturated capitalist horror, not generic chrome. The enemies are auditors with currency-symbol faces, ad-drones screaming slogans and data-wraiths that look like corrupted video.
  • Core loop: deploy → fight → die or evolve → salvage blueprints → mod gear → redeploy.
  • The x-factor, destructive installation: installing a blueprint consumes it. There are no take-backs. Your build is a record of every irreversible choice you made.

The World

The Federation is a body of member planets. Its public face is the Council of the Federation, which governs day to day. A world's seats on the Council are weighted by its worth (population, exports, economic output), so rich core worlds hold many seats and poor, sparse ones hold one or none. For a kid from a slum district, or someone pulled from a world outside the Federation entirely, the show is one of the few ladders up.

The arena is virtual

Operators jack in. The arena is a simulated world, and your six CPU attributes are your specs inside the system. Gear, wounds and blueprints are real to the system and to the audience.

Death in the arena

There is no respawn. If your operator is destroyed, the real person who jacked in survives, but the build is gone forever: character, unlocked nodes, installed blueprints and the blueprint stockpile are erased. You stake your investment, not your life. See Flatlined, p. {{pg:flatlined}}.

The condo

Between deployments operators live in a corporate tower: a private unit each, shared common areas, and a terminal in every room that owns you a little more each day. You choose, scene by scene, whether the cameras follow you home. See Chapter 8.

What You Need

  • Six-sided dice, lots of them. Ten per player is comfortable; checks roll pools of 1 to 10 dice.
  • A Game Master (GM) who runs the arena, the mobs, the corporation and the cameras.
  • An Operator Record for each player (in the Quick Reference PDF) or the P.A.T.C.H. companion app, which tracks vitals, AP, gear and the broadcast meter.
  • A few other dice for damage: d4, d8, d10 and d12.

How Play Works

Like every tabletop RPG, play is a conversation:

  1. The GM describes the scene: where you are, what you see, what the crowd is chanting.
  2. Players say what their operators do.
  3. The GM decides whether it needs a roll. If success is certain, or failure is impossible or boring, it simply happens. Otherwise the GM calls for a check and sets a target.
  4. The GM narrates the result, and the conversation moves on.

Combat is the exception: it runs in rounds and turns with an action economy (Chapter 5).

The Core Roll

One rule to learn first

Roll a pool of d6 equal to attribute + skill. Add the dice. Meet or beat the target to succeed.

Targets are fixed: 10 routine, 15 tricky, 20 hard, 25 heroic, 30 legendary. Every point over the target is flair, which makes better television.

Attributes run from 1 to 5 (3 is an average operator) and skills add ranks on top, so a pool runs from 1 die to 10. The targets never scale with you. Growing your pool is how you get reliably better. The full rules are in Chapter 4.

Example

Rook vaults a collapsing catwalk. The GM calls for PING + Acrobatics against 15. Rook has PING 4 and Acrobatics 2, so the player rolls 6d6: 3, 5, 2, 6, 4, 1 = 21. Success with 6 flair: the vault lands clean and the camera drone catches it in slow motion.

The Loop

A campaign alternates between two phases.

Deployment. Operators jack into an arena sector (difficulty 1 to 5) and fight through one or more encounters. They earn Data Fragments, salvage blueprints and build Engagement with the audience.

Condo Phase. Operators jack out. They heal, shop the terminal market, install blueprints, spend Advancement Points, and decide how much of their private life to sell to the cameras.

Then the next deployment begins. Stagnation is failure.
